The custody model

The platform is built around one pipeline:

  • Artifact
  • Version
  • Event
  • Evidence
  • Search
  • Snapshot
  • Certification
  • Provenance

The core object is the custody record, and everything else attaches to it:

  • Customer
  • Custody account
  • IP project
  • Artifact
  • Version
  • Evidence snapshot
  • Prior-art search
  • Matches

Put together, it covers Creation → Development → Research → Monitoring → Governance → Certification → Disclosure → Commercialization.

Research & prior art

Prior-art searching draws on public patent and research sources, where the respective services allow it: USPTO, Google Patents, EPO/Espacenet, WIPO and, at higher tiers, Lens, plus non-patent literature from OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, together with your own uploaded material.

IP intelligence (ATLAS)

  • Deep prior-art research and patent landscape analysis
  • Non-patent literature research
  • Patent-family maps and citation graphs
  • Inventor/entity relationships and technology classifications
  • Competitive and technology landscape
  • An evidence graph linking your IP → technical features → patents, papers, products → families, citations, entities

Feature-level matching

Atlas doesn't give you a single "87% similar" score. It maps individual technical features to what it found:

  • Directly supported
  • Potentially related
  • Unresolved
  • Not located

Search results are research aids, not legal opinions on novelty or patentability. Talk to a patent attorney before relying on them.

AI-assisted "Has this been done before?" check

After you upload an asset, an AI-assisted scan can look for similar public ideas and return the closest examples with links, plus a report saved to your vault. A basic search is planned from SEED, with deeper research from ATLAS. See the pricing page.

Start today for free. The Prior Art Search page opens your query on Google Patents, Espacenet, PATENTSCOPE, The Lens, Google Scholar, arXiv and other free public databases.

The 10-question consultation

Discovery

  1. What type of IP are you protecting? (patent, trademark, copyright, trade secret, other)
  2. What stage is it at? (concept, prototype, developed, market-ready)
  3. What is your primary goal? (protection, commercialization, licensing, defense)

Context

  1. What is your estimated budget for IP protection?
  2. What is your timeline for filing/registration?
  3. Have you filed for IP protection before?

Strategy

  1. Who are your main competitors or similar solutions?
  2. What makes your IP unique or innovative?
  3. Do you plan international protection?
  4. What support do you need most right now?

The output: an automated IP Readiness Score (0–100), recommended next steps, tier suggestions, and, if you opt in to the showcase, suggested law firms.

The consultation and any AI assistance give general information only. They are not legal advice and don't create an attorney-client relationship.

Security & compliance roadmap

Planned security architecture

  • Encrypted vault (AES-256) and end-to-end encryption for stored IP assets
  • Multi-factor authentication for all accounts
  • Zero-trust access model (every request validated)
  • Hardware security modules (HSM) for key management and multi-party computation (MPC) for sensitive operations
  • Immutable chain-of-custody logs for all IP access
  • Role-based access control with least privilege
  • Regular third-party security audits and automated compliance monitoring
  • No private keys handled in the first-upload pathway

Compliance targets

IP Custody is working toward these standards. None of them has been certified or audited yet.

  • SOC 2 Type II
  • ISO 27001 information security management
  • GDPR data protection
  • HIPAA alignment for healthcare-related IP
  • NIST 800-171 controls for defense contractors

Build roadmap

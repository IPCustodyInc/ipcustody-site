1. Understanding your Certificate of Existence

Your Certificate of Existence is blockchain-anchored evidence that you had a specific piece of intellectual property on a specific date.

What it shows

  • You had this IP asset on the recorded date
  • The content hasn't been altered since the timestamp
  • Supporting evidence of prior art and of your conception date
  • Cryptographic evidence you and your attorney can present in legal proceedings (admissibility is up to the court)

What it does NOT replace

  • Patent applications (file with the USPTO)
  • Copyright registration (U.S. Copyright Office, needed for statutory damages)
  • Trademark registration (USPTO, for brand protection)
  • Legal advice from an attorney

2. How blockchain timestamps work

  1. Hashing

    Your file (document, image, code, etc.) is converted into a unique cryptographic fingerprint (hash). Even one tiny change creates a completely different hash.

  2. Blockchain recording

    The hash is recorded on a public blockchain with a timestamp. Public blockchains are built so records can't be altered or deleted after the fact.

  3. Verification

    Anyone can compare your file's current hash with the blockchain record. Matching hashes show the file existed unchanged at that time.

Why this matters

  • Tamper-evident: blockchain records can't be changed retroactively.
  • Transparent: anyone can verify them independently.
  • Recognized by courts: courts have relied on blockchain timestamps as evidence, including the Tribunal judiciaire de Marseille in March 2025. See legal validity.

3. Legal value & how to use your certificate

Federal Rules of Evidence

  • FRE 901 (Authentication): your certificate is designed to help show that the IP asset is what you claim it is.
  • FRE 902(13)/(14) (Self-authentication): certain machine-generated records and hash-identified data can be authenticated with a qualified person's certification, which may reduce the need for live witness testimony.

Key precedent

In March 2025 the Tribunal judiciaire de Marseille (France) relied on Bitcoin-anchored blockchain timestamps, together with other evidence, to establish copyright ownership from the recorded dates in a fashion infringement case.

How to use your certificate

  • Patent applications: share with your attorney as documentation of your conception date, and to support provisional filings.
  • Copyright claims: show the creation date of unregistered works and support priority in infringement matters.
  • Trade secret protection: document confidential information and support misappropriation and NDA claims.
  • Litigation: give your attorney a ready exhibit for your development timeline and prior-art questions.

4. Next steps for IP protection

Immediate (this week)

  • Save your certificate in multiple secure places (cloud + local)
  • Document your IP development process
  • Decide what formal protection you need (patent, copyright, trademark)
  • Review the Legal Toolkit included with your subscription

Short term (this month)

  • Talk to an IP attorney about formal options
  • File a provisional patent if you're inventing (12-month window)
  • Register copyright if you're creating
  • Put NDAs in place with collaborators

Ongoing

  • Timestamp major updates and iterations
  • Keep detailed development logs
  • Monitor for infringement
  • Keep your IP Custody subscription active for vault access

5. Working with patent attorneys

When to hire

  • You're ready to file a patent application
  • You've found potential infringement
  • You're negotiating licensing deals
  • You need to enforce your IP rights

What to bring

  • Your Certificate of Existence
  • A detailed description of the invention/creation
  • Your development timeline and records
  • Prior-art search results (if you have them; start with our free Prior Art Search)
  • NDAs or collaborator agreements

Key questions to ask

  • "What type of IP protection is best for my assets?"
  • "What is the timeline and cost for filing?"
  • "How does my blockchain certificate strengthen my case?"
  • "What risks exist if I don't file formal protection?"

FAQ

What it proves, and what it doesn't

Straight answers about Certificates of Existence, blockchain timestamps, privacy, pricing and where IP Custody stands today.

What it proves

  • A specific file existed at the recorded time
  • The file hasn't changed since (hash match)
  • An independent, public time reference once anchored
  • Supporting evidence of your development timeline

What it doesn't

  • Ownership, authorship or inventorship
  • Novelty, patentability or infringement
  • A patent, copyright or trademark registration
  • Legal advice, or any guaranteed outcome in court
What does a Certificate of Existence prove?

It shows that a specific file, identified by its SHA-256 hash, existed at the recorded time and hasn't changed since. Once the record is anchored to a public blockchain, anyone can check that independently.

What doesn't it prove?

It doesn't prove that you are the author or owner, that your idea is new or patentable, or that someone infringed your rights. It doesn't replace patent applications, copyright registration, trademark registration or advice from an attorney. IP Custody keeps technical evidence separate from legal conclusions.

Will my certificate hold up in court?

Courts have accepted blockchain timestamps as evidence. For example, in March 2025 the Tribunal judiciaire de Marseille relied on Bitcoin-anchored timestamps, together with other evidence, to establish copyright ownership. In the U.S., certificates are designed to support authentication under Federal Rules of Evidence 901 and 902(13)/(14). But admissibility and weight are always up to the judge, and a timestamp works best alongside other evidence like NDAs, emails and version history. We can't guarantee any outcome. Read more.

Is my file uploaded to the blockchain?

No. Only a cryptographic fingerprint (hash), or a Merkle root that combines many hashes, is anchored. Your confidential file stays off-chain in your encrypted vault or on your own device, and nobody can reconstruct it from the hash.

Does the Hash & Proof Tool upload my file?

No. The Hash & Proof Tool runs entirely in your browser using the built-in Web Crypto API. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere, and nothing is written to a blockchain. The receipt it creates is self-generated: it is not a blockchain timestamp or an IP Custody certificate.

Is the Prior Art Search a patentability or clearance opinion?

No. The Prior Art Search page simply opens your query on free public databases run by other organizations. It doesn't analyze results, and it is not a novelty, patentability, freedom-to-operate or trademark clearance opinion. Your query is sent to each site you open, so avoid typing confidential details. Ask a registered patent attorney or agent before relying on any search.

Which blockchain do you use?

The current prototype prepares anchors for Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps (OP_RETURN). The planned architecture adds optional fast anchoring (for example Polygon) and multi-chain checkpoints on Ethereum, Polygon, Solana and Bitcoin at higher tiers.

What if IP Custody disappears?

Anchors made with OpenTimestamps can be verified against the public Bitcoin blockchain with your proof file, without relying on IP Custody or any calendar server. Keep copies of your original files and certificates in more than one safe place.

How is this different from emailing a file to myself or a notary?

Self-emailed or dated files are easy to dispute, and notaries or traditional timestamp authorities are central parties you have to trust. A public blockchain anchor has no single point of failure and can be checked by anyone.

Can I use it for AI-generated work?

Yes. You can timestamp prompts and outputs together as a record of your creative process. Keep in mind that the U.S. Copyright Office requires human authorship for copyright, so ask an attorney how that applies to your work.

Should I still file a patent, copyright or trademark?

For the ideas that matter, yes. The recommended path is layered: timestamp on Day 0, use NDAs in week 1, register copyright or file a provisional patent in month 1, and trademark your brand in months 3–6. See the comparisons.
